Useful when

The builder is too familiar with the work to be the only person checking it.

A website change, digital document, workflow, form or tool-assisted deliverable may be functionally complete and still contain confusing wording, broken states, inconsistency or unsupported claims.

The review is defined against the thing being released. It does not pretend to be certification or take accountability away from the organisation releasing it.

What you receive

  • Review against agreed purpose, audience and requirements.
  • Content, logic, consistency and usability checks relevant to the item.
  • Unsupported-claim and ambiguity flags.
  • Prioritised defect list.
  • Release recommendation against the agreed review scope.

What it does not replace

  • Security penetration testing.
  • Legal sign-off or regulated certification.
  • Specialist formal accessibility audit where one is required.
  • The customer's release ownership and decision.
  • A guarantee that no defect can remain.
Quote basis

Tell us what is changing and what failure would matter.

The number of artefacts, routes or states and the depth of assurance determine the scope. A small bounded release can be priced directly; larger or higher-risk work needs a written quote.
