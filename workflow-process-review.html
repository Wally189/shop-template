The situation

Everyone knows the workaround. Nobody is quite sure why it is necessary.

A process may have grown through spreadsheets, inboxes, duplicated checks, inherited tools and decisions that only one person understands. The review makes the current flow visible before prescribing a solution.

Automation may be one option, but it is not the default answer. Sometimes the useful improvement is simply a cleaner sequence, clearer ownership or fewer unnecessary steps.

What you receive

  • Current-state workflow or equivalent clear representation.
  • Friction, waiting, duplication and failure points.
  • Ownership and decision issues that materially affect the work.
  • Simplified future-state recommendation.
  • Practical action list and proportionate automation opportunities where useful.

Boundaries

  • One bounded process unless otherwise quoted.
  • No organisation-wide transformation programme.
  • No employment or legal restructuring advice.
  • No software implementation hidden inside the review.
  • No claim of savings until real evidence exists.
First step

Name the recurring job, not the tool you think you need.

Tell us what starts the process, what “finished” means, who is involved and where it seems to get stuck. That is enough to establish whether a bounded review is sensible.
