Working method

Good professional work is easier to trust when you can see where the boundary is.

House of Carol does not begin with a favourite tool or a standard answer. The first task is to understand what needs to be achieved, what is known, what remains uncertain and whether the work can be bounded sensibly.

01 · Orient

Understand first.

A good engagement begins with the actual job rather than the language used to sell it. We clarify the desired result, the material available, the people or decisions involved and the constraints that matter.

02 · Bound

Agree the boundary.

Scope is not small print added after the sale. It should be understandable before work starts: what is included, what is not, what the customer needs to provide, what House of Carol will return and what would require a change of plan.

03 · Evidence

Work from what can be supported.

Facts, assumptions and judgement are not the same thing. Where evidence is incomplete, the work should say so rather than manufacture confidence. Where a conclusion matters, the reasoning behind it should be recoverable.

04 · Proportion

Use technology proportionately.

Digital tools can remove effort, organise information and support analysis. They are used where they improve the result, not because every piece of work needs a technological performance. Human judgement and accountability remain with the people responsible for the work.

05 · Check

Check the actual result.

Quality is not a decorative claim. The relevant deliverable is checked against what was agreed, including awkward or failure cases where the nature of the work requires them.

06 · Return

Hand it back properly.

A customer should not need House of Carol indefinitely simply because House of Carol did the original work. The handover should be understandable, responsibilities should be clear and any remaining limitations should be visible.

07 · Remedy

When something goes wrong.

Mistakes, misunderstandings and complaints should have a route to correction. House of Carol maintains a complaints and remedy process so a problem can be described, reviewed and answered rather than hidden behind vague service language.
