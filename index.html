Important business work, given proper attention.

House of Carol takes on defined pieces of work that matter to small organisations — jobs that need time, distance or careful judgement, but do not always justify another permanent role.

The work starts with the job itself: what needs doing, what information exists, what a useful result would look like, and whether the House is the right place to do it.

The situation

Some work becomes harder simply because you are too close to it.

A useful outside view is not about adding another layer of consultancy. It is about seeing the job clearly enough to decide what matters next.

01

An important submission has been read so many times that nobody can see it freshly.

02

A piece of information is technically complete but still difficult to make a decision from.

03

A defined job keeps slipping between people because it belongs to everyone and no one.

House of Carol is designed for bounded work like this: understand the problem, agree what a useful outcome means, do the work carefully and hand back something you can use.

Current work

Tender Review

A fresh pair of eyes before an important submission goes in.

Tender Review is for a substantially complete tender that needs an independent final challenge before submission. The review looks at what the tender asks for, how the response answers it, whether important claims are supported, and which remaining weaknesses deserve attention first.

It is a review, not a promise of winning and not a hidden bid-writing engagement.

Working approach

Clear before clever.

We establish the job before prescribing the answer. Scope and responsibility stay visible. Important conclusions should have a trail back to the material behind them.

Understand the exact job

Start with what needs to be achieved, what is known and what remains uncertain.

Agree the boundary

Make clear what is included, what is not, what the customer provides and what the House returns.

Work from what can be supported

Separate fact, assumption and judgement rather than manufacturing confidence.

Hand back something usable

The customer should understand what was done, what remains theirs to decide and what to do next.
